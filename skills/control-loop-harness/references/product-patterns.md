# Product And Outcome Patterns

Load this reference when the concrete question or an evidenced candidate concerns product scope, depth, fidelity, roughness, or completion.

Treat a closed loop as evidence that a path works, not proof that the intended product effect is deep or complete enough. Recover the relevant quality and scope from the approved target rather than applying a universal product checklist.

## Experienced Patterns

- **Minimum-loop compression**: confirm whether the approved effect included breadth or depth that the current loop omits. Do not infer broader scope solely because a richer product seems desirable.
- **MVP laundering**: look for unapproved introduction of MVP, P0, proof, demo, or thin-slice framing as the final boundary.
- **Literal obedience**: compare the literal request with its explicit product meaning, artifacts, and acceptance evidence; do not claim hidden intent without support.
- **Goal shrink**: trace an approved capability or quality bar across target, plan, and implementation.
- **False completion**: distinguish evidence and prerequisites from the user-visible outcome they were meant to support.
- **Layer omission**: inspect only layers required by the actual capability, such as interaction, logic, state, record, trust, feedback, or next action.
- **Hard-coded intelligence**: verify whether the result was meant to prove a real judgment chain rather than a presentation-only fixture.
- **Fidelity collapse**: compare implementation with an approved prototype, prompt, contract, or other canonical artifact.
- **Trust-gap roughness**: determine whether a missing user-control or safety capability was part of the promise rather than optional polish.

Distinguish acceptable roughness from under-building using the approved effect and current evidence. Recommend the smallest increase in depth that restores that effect; do not use "minimum" to redefine it.

If the evidence reveals a recurring product failure not covered here, describe it as a provisional new pattern and explain how recognizing it would improve future work.
